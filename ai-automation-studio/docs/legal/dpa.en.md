## 1. Law that applies

1.1. **Data protection law** means the Law of Georgia "On Personal Data Protection" [VERIFY: adopted
14.06.2023, in force since 01.03.2024], and any other data protection law that applies to the Client,
for example the EU General Data Protection Regulation (GDPR) for clients in the EU.

1.2. **Supervisory authority** means [LAWYER: name the current authority in Georgia that supervises
personal data protection].

1.3. If a law other than Georgian law applies to the Client, the Client tells the Developer before
signing. The parties then add what that law requires.

## 2. Roles

2.1. The Client is the controller of the personal data processed with the system. The Developer is
the processor.

2.2. The Developer processes personal data only to provide the Services and only on the Client's
documented instructions. The instructions are: the Agreement, the Order Forms, the Configuration,
and the Client's written requests (email or messenger messages are enough; for a marketplace
client, only messages on the marketplace).

2.3. If the Developer believes an instruction breaks the law, it tells the Client and may pause that
instruction until the Client confirms or changes it.

2.4. The Developer does not use the Client's personal data for its own purposes. It does not sell it,
does not use it to train AI models, and does not combine it with other clients' data.

## 3. What is processed

The subject matter, purpose, nature and duration of processing, the categories of people and of
data, and where data is stored, are set out in **Annex 1**.

## 4. Confidentiality

4.1. The Developer works alone. No other person on the Developer's side has access to the Client's
personal data. Sub-processors process it only as set in section 6.

4.2. If anyone else will need access, for example a substitute during a long absence, the Developer
tells the Client in advance. That person must be bound by confidentiality before getting access.

## 5. Security

5.1. The Developer applies the measures in **Annex 2**.

5.2. The Developer may improve these measures. It will not lower the overall level of protection.

## 6. Sub-processors

6.1. The Client authorises the Developer to use the sub-processors listed in **Annex 3**.

6.2. To add or replace a sub-processor, the Developer tells the Client by email (for a marketplace
client, only through the marketplace's messages) at least **30 days** in advance. If a sub-processor
must be replaced urgently, for security or because it stops its service, the Developer tells the
Client as soon as possible.

6.3. The Client may object within 14 days on reasonable data protection grounds. The parties then
look for a solution. If there is none, the Client may end the affected Services without penalty. The
Developer refunds prepaid fees for the period after the end.

6.4. The Developer uses sub-processors under their standard business terms, which include data
protection terms. The Developer cannot negotiate these terms. With this DPA, the Developer gives
the Client links to the current terms of each sub-processor. The Developer remains responsible to
the Client for choosing and setting up its sub-processors.

## 8. Requests from people

8.1. If a person asks the Developer directly to see, correct, delete or export their data, the
Developer forwards the request to the Client within 2 Business Days (for a marketplace client,
through the marketplace's messages). The Developer does not answer
it itself unless the Client asks.

8.2. Within 10 Business Days of the Client's request, the Developer finds, exports, corrects or
deletes a person's data held on its server. This is part of support and uses no plan hours.

8.3. Data in Client Accounts, such as the Google Sheet or the mailbox, is under the Client's control.
The Client can change it directly. The Developer helps if asked.

## 9. Personal data breaches

9.1. A **personal data breach** is a security incident that leads to personal data being lost,
destroyed, changed, disclosed or accessed without authorisation.

9.2. The Developer tells the Client **without undue delay, and no later than 24 hours** after
becoming aware of a breach that affects the Client's personal data. The notice says what is known at
that point:
- what happened and when;
- which categories of data and people are affected, and roughly how many;
- the likely consequences;
- what the Developer has done and proposes to do;
- how to contact the Developer.

The Developer sends more information as it becomes available.

9.3. The Client decides whether to notify the supervisory authority and the people affected. Under
the Law of Georgia "On Personal Data Protection", the controller notifies the supervisory authority
within **72 hours** of becoming aware of a breach, except where the law says otherwise [VERIFY: the
deadline and its exceptions]. The 24-hour notice in 9.2 gives the Client time to meet this deadline.
The Developer helps the Client prepare the notifications.

9.4. The Developer does not notify the authority or the people affected on the Client's behalf,
unless the Client asks or the law requires it.

9.5. The Developer keeps a record of every breach: the facts, the effects and the measures taken.

## 10. Help with compliance

10.1. The Developer gives the Client the information it reasonably needs to show compliance and to
assess the risks of the processing.

10.2. Once a year, the Developer answers the Client's written questionnaire about the processing,
free of charge.

10.3. Further audits are at the Client's cost, by agreement. The Client gives 30 days' notice. Audits
take place in business hours, under confidentiality, and without access to other clients' data.

## 11. Retention and deletion

11.1. Each kind of data is kept for the period set in **Annex 1**. Data in Client Accounts is kept
for as long as the Client decides.

11.2. When the Services end:
- if the Client asks within 30 days, the Developer exports the Client's data held on its server, in
  CSV or another common format;
- within **30 days** after the end, the Developer deletes the Client's personal data from its
  servers and backups;
- on request, the Developer confirms the deletion in writing.

11.3. The Developer keeps only the records the law requires it to keep, such as invoices and this
agreement. They contain the Client's business details, not the personal data of the Client's
customers.

## Annex 2. Security measures

**Separation**
- Each client has a separate deployment: its own configuration, data directory and process. One
  client's data is never stored in another client's database.

**Secrets**
- Passwords, API keys and tokens are kept only in an environment file on the server, readable only by
  the service user, and in the Developer's encrypted password manager.
- They are never put in configuration files, code repositories, logs or error messages. The code
  strips tokens from error texts.
- Clients' configurations and secrets are not stored in the code repository.

**Access to the server and accounts**
- Only the Developer has access to the server.
- Login by SSH key only. Password login is disabled. The firewall allows incoming SSH only, plus
  HTTPS if a WhatsApp webhook is enabled. The Telegram booking bot connects out to Telegram and
  needs no open ports.
- Two-factor authentication on every account used for the Services: hosting, AI provider, Google,
  Telegram, email.
- A separate AI provider key per client where practical, so one key can be revoked without
  affecting others. A spending limit is set with the AI provider.
- The Developer's computers use full-disk encryption, a screen lock and current security updates.

**Least access to Client Accounts**
- Google Sheet: shared only with the Developer's service account, as editor of that one sheet.
- Google Calendar, if enabled: shared with the service account with permission to make changes to
  events.
- Mailbox: an app password, or OAuth access granted by the Client's administrator. The Client can
  withdraw it at any time.
- WhatsApp: the Developer is added as an admin or system user in the Client's business portfolio.

**Data minimisation, built into the code**
- Booking bot: conversations are not stored. The event log holds no names or phone numbers. The
  customer's name and phone are not sent to the AI provider.
- Inbox: email bodies and attachments are not stored. Extracted fields are erased once the row is
  written to the sheet. Processing records are deleted after 90 days. Newsletters, auto-replies and
  hidden HTML text are not sent to the AI provider.
- Price monitor: no personal data is collected. Requests to sites never include the Client's name.

**AI providers**
- Each task sends the AI provider only what it needs (Annex 1).
- Requests to OpenAI are sent with storage of the response turned off (`store=false`).
- Under their API terms, the providers state that API data is not used to train their models by
  default [VERIFY: on the date of signing, and attach the links]. They may keep data for a limited
  time for safety and abuse monitoring, as described in their terms.

**Checks on AI output**
- Every AI answer must match a fixed format, or it is rejected.
- A phone number, email or budget that is not in the source is thrown out.
- Reply drafts are never sent automatically. A booking is made only when the customer presses
  "Confirm". A price is accepted only if the AI's quote is found on the page.
- An email that tries to give orders to the AI is flagged in the sheet. No draft, folder copy or
  alert to the Client is made for it, and the Developer is alerted.

**Spreadsheet protection**
- Values that start with `=`, `+`, `-` or `@` are written so that the sheet does not run them as
  formulas.

**Backups, updates and logs**
- [VERIFY: backups.] Daily backups to separate storage, encrypted, kept for up to 30 days.
- Monthly updates of software components, with automated tests before each deployment.
- Monitoring alerts the Developer when a run fails or stops. Technical logs are rotated by size and
  deleted at each redeployment, at least once a month.

**Breaches**
- As set in section 9 of this DPA.
