## 1. What this agreement covers

1.1. The Developer provides **software development, configuration, integration and maintenance
services** (the **Services**). The Developer configures its ready-made automation software for
the Client's business, connects it to the Client's accounts, hosts it and maintains it.

1.2. Each piece of work is set in an **Order Form** signed by both parties (template in
Schedule 1). An Order Form names:
- the package: Inbox, Booking bot or Price monitor;
- the tier: Basic, Standard or Premium;
- the price and the delivery time;
- the support plan, if any: Care, Pro or Business.

What each tier includes is described in the Developer's price list dated [date]. The price list is
attached to the Order Form.

1.3. The Services are not consulting. They are not legal, tax, accounting, financial or marketing
advice. [ACCOUNTANT: confirm that this wording describes software development and not "consulting"
excluded from the small business regime.]

1.4. Two more documents are part of this agreement:
- the **Support Terms**: how support works;
- the **Data Processing Agreement** (**DPA**): how personal data is handled.

1.5. If these documents say different things:
- on personal data, the DPA wins;
- on scope, price and dates, the Order Form wins;
- on support, the Support Terms win;
- on everything else, this agreement wins.

1.6. This agreement is made between businesses. It is not made with consumers.

## 3. What the Client provides

3.1. The Client provides, on time and accurately, everything listed under "What you provide" in the
price list for its package and tier. For example: services and opening hours, FAQ answers, company
facts, product links, access to Client Accounts.

3.2. The delivery time starts when the Developer has both the prepayment and everything in 3.1.
Time spent waiting for others is not counted. For example: Meta reviewing message templates, or the
Client's Google Workspace administrator granting access.

3.3. The Client is responsible for the facts it provides: prices, opening hours, policies, FAQ
answers. The bot and the reply drafts rely on them.

## 4. Delivery, revisions and acceptance

4.1. The Developer delivers within the delivery time in the Order Form, counted in Business Days.

4.2. Before Launch, the Client gets two rounds of changes within its tier: texts, categories, FAQ,
alert settings. Anything else is a change request (section 6).

4.3. **Acceptance.** The Developer shows the system working on the Client's real data [or on test
data agreed with the Client]. Then the Developer tells the Client in writing that the work is ready.
The Client has **5 Business Days** to report in writing anything that does not work as described in
the Order Form. The Developer fixes what was reported and shows it again. The Client then has
5 Business Days to check those items only.

4.4. If the Client reports nothing within 5 Business Days, the work is accepted. A small defect that
does not stop the system from being used does not block acceptance. The Developer fixes it under
section 5.

4.5. If the Client hired the Developer through an online marketplace, such as Upwork or Fiverr,
the marketplace's acceptance rules apply where they differ from 4.3 and 4.4.

## 5. Free fixes

5.1. After Launch, the Developer fixes for free anything that does not work as described in the
Order Form:
- for 14 days for Basic and Standard;
- for 30 days for Premium.

5.2. After that, the Developer fixes its own bugs for free while the Client has an active support
plan. All other support work follows the Support Terms.

## 6. Change requests

6.1. Anything that is not in the Order Form is a change request. For example: a new feature, a new
integration or channel, a new data source, a limit above the tier.

6.2. The Developer estimates the hours first. Work starts only after the Client approves the
estimate in writing. An email or a messenger message is enough; for a marketplace client, only a
message on the marketplace. The work is paid at the hourly rate
in the Order Form, in 30-minute steps. Larger work can instead get a fixed price in a new Order
Form.

7.7. **Price changes.**
- A setup price in a signed Order Form does not change.

## 9. Use of AI

9.1. The system uses AI models from the provider named in the Order Form (Anthropic or OpenAI) for
some tasks:
- understanding messages that customers type to the bot;
- sorting emails and writing reply drafts;
- reading prices from pages that have no price markup.

The Basic booking bot uses no AI.

9.2. AI can make mistakes. So the system is built on one rule: **AI suggests, code checks.**
- Reply drafts are never sent automatically.
- A booking is made only when the customer presses "Confirm".
- A price read by AI is accepted only if the AI's quote is found on the page and gives the same
  number.
- A phone number, email or budget that is not in the email is not saved.

The Client reviews each draft before sending it. The Client is responsible for what it sends.

9.3. The Developer does not hide the use of AI. If the Client wants a task done without AI, the
Client says so before the Order Form is signed. The Inbox package does not work without AI.

## 11. What the Client promises

- as a general-purpose AI chatbot;
- to collect health data, diagnoses or other special categories of personal data. A clinic's

11.3. **Countries and sanctions.** The Client and the people who use the system are not located in a

Client is not subject to sanctions that prohibit the Developer from working with it.

11.4. **Price monitor.** If the Order Form includes the Price monitor:
- (a) **Purpose.** The Client uses the collected prices for a lawful internal purpose: its own
  pricing and product decisions. The Client does not republish the content of the monitored pages.
- (b) **Public pages only.** The monitor reads only public pages that anyone can see without logging
  in or paying. The Developer does not get around CAPTCHAs, bot protection or paywalls, and does
  not use proxies to hide the monitor.
- (c) **Choice of sites.** The Client chooses the links. The Client confirms in writing that, as far
  as it knows, the terms of each site allow monitoring those pages. The Client is responsible to the
  site owners for this choice and for how it uses the prices.
- (d) **No personal data.** The monitor collects prices, currency and availability. It does not
  collect personal data, such as seller names on marketplaces.
- (e) **Polite collection.** The Developer follows each site's robots.txt rules, and departs from
  them only with the site owner's written permission. The monitor waits at least 5 seconds between
  requests to the same site, and longer if the site's robots.txt asks for a delay (up to 60
  seconds). Each request names the monitor and gives a contact email, so a site owner can reach the
  Developer. The Client's name is never in the requests.
- (f) **Stop on complaint.** If a site owner objects or blocks the monitor, or if the Developer
  reasonably believes that monitoring a site breaks its terms or the law, the Developer stops
  monitoring that site on the same day and tells the Client. This is not a breach of this agreement.
  The Client may replace the site with another one within its tier's limits.

11.5. **Inbox.** The Client controls the connected mailbox and allows the Developer to connect to it.
The Client can withdraw this access at any time. The system does not send or delete emails. The
Client reviews every draft before sending it.

11.6. **WhatsApp.** If the Order Form includes WhatsApp, the Client owns the WhatsApp Business
account and the Meta business portfolio. The Client keeps a valid payment card in WhatsApp Manager,
approves the consent (opt-in) text, and follows Meta's WhatsApp Business rules. Meta allows a
business's own booking and FAQ bot. Meta does not allow general-purpose AI assistants.

13.1. **Cap.** Each party's total liability under this agreement and all Order Forms is limited to
the fees the Client paid the Developer in the **3 months** before the event that caused the claim.

13.2. Neither party is liable for lost profit, lost revenue, lost bookings or leads, or other
indirect losses.

13.3. 13.1 and 13.2 do not limit:
- the Client's duty to pay the fees;
- liability for intentional breach or gross negligence;
- any liability that the law does not allow to limit.

13.4. The Developer is not liable for:
- Third-Party Services, as set in 8.3;
- the Client's instructions, data or facts;
- the Client withdrawing access to Client Accounts;
- emails and messages the Client sends after reviewing a draft;
- changes the Client makes to Client Accounts, for example renaming a tab in the Google Sheet.

13.5. Each party is responsible for claims by third parties that its own breach of this agreement or
of the law has caused.

13.6. **Force majeure.** Neither party is liable for a delay or failure caused by events outside its
reasonable control, such as a natural disaster, war, a government act, or a large-scale outage of
the internet or of a Third-Party Service. The party affected tells the other promptly. If the event
lasts more than 30 days, either party may end the affected Order Form in writing.

## 14. Confidentiality

14.1. Each party keeps confidential the other party's non-public information that it receives under
this agreement: business data, prices, customer information, access details, and the source code of
the Framework.

14.2. This does not apply to information that is public through no fault of the receiving party, was
already known to it, was received lawfully from someone else, or must be disclosed by law or a court.
In the last case, the party tells the other first, if the law allows.

14.3. This duty lasts while this agreement is in force and for 3 years after it ends. For personal
data and access details it lasts for as long as the information is held.

## 15. Term and termination

15.1. This agreement starts when both parties sign it. It continues while any Order Form or support
plan is active.

15.2. **Cancelling a setup before acceptance.** The Client may cancel a setup at any time in
writing. The Developer keeps the part of the prepayment that covers the work already done, counted
in hours at the hourly rate, and shows its time log. The Developer returns the rest. If the Developer
misses the delivery time by more than 10 Business Days for reasons under its control, the Client may
cancel and get the whole prepayment back.

15.3. **Support plans.** The Client may end a support plan at any time in writing. The service runs
to the end of the paid month. Partial months are not refunded. The Developer may end a support plan
with 30 days' written notice. In that case the Developer refunds any prepaid days after the end.

15.4. **Breach.** Either party may end this agreement in writing if the other party does not fix a
material breach within 14 days after written notice. Late support payments are handled in the
Support Terms.

15.5. **When the Services end:**
- (a) If the Client asks within 30 days after the end, the Developer exports the Client Data held on
  its server, in CSV or another common format, together with the Configuration.
- (b) The Developer removes its own access to Client Accounts. It asks the Client to withdraw the
  passwords, tokens and sharing it gave the Developer. For example: revoke the app password, issue a
  new bot token in @BotFather, stop sharing the Google Sheet.
- (c) The Developer deletes Client Data from its servers and backups within 30 days after the end, as
  set in the DPA, and confirms this in writing on request.
- (d) The Client's rights under 10.3, confidentiality (14), liability (13) and unpaid fees survive
  the end of this agreement.
